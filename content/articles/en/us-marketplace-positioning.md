Most brands entering the U.S. marketplace assume their challenge is translation. They rewrite their listings in fluent English, hire a native copywriter, and wait for conversion to climb. It rarely does.

## The real problem is category framing

A U.S. shopper doesn't evaluate your product against your intentions — they evaluate it against the category they think it belongs to. If your framing puts you in the wrong category, even perfect copy competes on the wrong axis.

- **Wrong category, right copy** loses to the shopper's default expectation.
- **Right category, average copy** wins because it answers the question the shopper is actually asking.

## What to fix first

1. Identify the category the shopper *defaults* to when they see your product.
2. Decide whether to compete inside it or deliberately reposition out of it.
3. Rebuild the listing around that decision — title, imagery, and the first three bullets.

Positioning is a decision about which comparison you want to win, made before a single word is written. Get the comparison right, and the rest of the listing finally has something to stand on.
